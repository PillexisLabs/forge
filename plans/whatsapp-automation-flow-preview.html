WhatsApp automation, explained simply

One page, no jargon. What it does, who does what, and the three pieces we still have to build.

A client books a call from your ad. Forge chats with them on WhatsApp — confirms the booking, reminds them before the call, handles reschedules — so they actually show up. You only step in when a real human is needed.

Today vs. after

The CRM you have now already knows what to send and when. The difference is who presses send.

Today — you are the automation

Forge is a checklist

  • Forge shows: "Riya needs a confirmation message."
  • You open WhatsApp on your phone and type it.
  • Riya replies to your phone. You read it.
  • You come back to Forge and click "Confirm" so the record stays true.
  • Forge computes "reminder due in 2h" — and then nothing happens unless you send it.

After — Forge is the automation

Forge does the chatting

  • Booking arrives → Forge sends the confirmation by itself.
  • Riya's reply lands inside Forge, on her record.
  • "Confirm" → reminders schedule themselves. "STOP" → messaging ends forever. "Can we do Monday?" → Forge sends the calendar link.
  • You look at one screen a day: the leads that need a human.

When does a human step in?

Only three situations. Everything else runs itself.

💬
The client asks something real

"What would this cost for two warehouses?" — Forge never answers pricing. It pauses, tells you why, and you take over the thread.

🔇
The client goes silent

Two unanswered nudges → Forge sends two gentle value messages, then stops for good. No spam, no chasing.

🛑
The client says STOP

Messaging ends permanently and automatically. This one isn't even work — you just see it in the record.

Why it doesn't work yet: 3 missing pieces

The brain is built — Forge already tracks states, consent, and exact send times. It's missing hands, a clock, and ears.

🖐️

Hands — a WhatsApp sender

A connection to WhatsApp's business system (Meta Cloud API), so Forge can actually deliver a message. Think of it as giving Forge its own SIM card.

⏰

A clock — the minute-check

A tiny job that wakes every minute and asks: "Is any message due now?" If yes — check consent one more time, then send. This is the actual "automation".

👂

Ears — an inbox

Replies must land inside Forge, on the lead's record — not on your personal phone. That's what lets "confirm" and "STOP" trigger the rules.

Build order: ① a few safety fixes so consent can never be bypassed → ② hands + clock (messages start sending) → ③ ears (replies come in) → ④ the silent-lead rules → ⑤ AI for free-form replies. Steps ①–③ are the whole demo.
